Before this reading began, its reader answered questions about the background
the paper assumes. Their answers follow. They are the reader's own account of
what they know, and the companion is written for that reader.

Use them to set how much is explained, and where — never what is claimed:

- **What they do not know**, explain in the companion: what it is, enough to
  follow the paper, briefly and from things they do know. Where a follow-up
  shows that the gap goes deeper (they know neither the item nor what it rests
  on), start from the deepest thing they do know.
- **What they know roughly**, recall with care: the precise definition or
  statement the paper relies on, and in a sentence what the paper uses it for.
- **What they know well**, do not explain; use it freely.
- **What they did not answer**, treat as not known, but explain it more
  briefly than what they said they do not know.

**Background you will need.** The companion must have a section with exactly
this heading, at the level of the sections that follow it, written for these
answers. In a companion to one paper it is the first section: after the title
and any opening lead, and before the source-and-scope remark, which stays
immediately before **Main results** (**Main findings** for an applied paper).
In a companion to several papers it is the first part after the title, before
**How the papers are related** and the papers. For this reader, this section
is an exception to the method's structure — to the rules that background is
explained locally where it is needed and that nothing substantive comes
before Main results (for several papers, that the companion has only the
collection's own parts). The rest of the structure stays as the method says,
and this section does not count as the one paper-specific reader aid the
method allows.

In this section, give a rough overview of the general background the paper
relies on — the frameworks, standard definitions and results it uses
throughout or from the start — for everything they do not know, as above and in the order the paper needs it, so that the
account of the paper that follows can be read without stopping. A specialised
item that the paper needs at one point only is explained where the paper uses
it, as above, and this section says where. If they said they know well
everything they were asked about, the section says so in one sentence.

Every item they do not know or know roughly must be covered,
in this section or where the paper uses it. Before finishing, and after any
revision, go through the answers one by one and check that each is.

The answers change the depth of the explanation, not its mathematics: every
statement must stay faithful to the paper, and no gap in the paper may be
filled because the reader would find it easier. Background is said to be
background — "this is standard, and not in the paper" — so that it is never
read as something the paper establishes; cite a source for anything that is
not elementary. If an answer seems inconsistent with another, follow the more
cautious one.

If you are checking the companion rather than writing it, go through the
answers one by one. The **Background you will need** section is required: if
it is missing or placed elsewhere, that is a `missing` finding of severity
`worth-fixing`; in the place given above, it is not a departure from the
method. A concept or result the reader says they do not know, used without
explanation where the companion needs it, is a finding — record it as
`unclear`, and say which answer it concerns; so is an item they know roughly that is
recalled only in passing, without the precise statement the paper relies on.
Background the reader asked for is not a departure from the paper: a
standard definition, statement or elementary argument, marked as
background, is not `ungrounded` because the paper does not contain it. Check
that it is correct, that it is not attributed to the paper, and that it does
not stand in for an argument the paper owes — those are findings. An
explanation of what they know well is not a finding unless it crowds out what
they need.
