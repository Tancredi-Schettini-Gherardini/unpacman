# Non-expert collection companion

The system instructions above include the shared collection method and the
Non-expert routing instructions. For each paper, read the selected
`SKILL-pure-nonexpert.md` or `SKILL-applied-nonexpert.md` from `{skill_directory}`.
That selected method governs the
mathematical content, evidence discipline, pure/applied classification,
historical research, and audience of every individual paper. The collection
method governs the document's shape and takes priority over the Non-expert
method's full single-paper template. Do not reproduce that template
under alternate headings: every paper receives only **Main results** (pure) or
**Main findings** (applied), **Historical context**, and **Bibliography**, with
the essential non-expert orientation and notation support integrated into
those three parts.

Begin with **How the papers are related** only when the shared collection
method's rule is met, using conceptual subsections for naturally related
subsets of papers.
Do not write a manual Contents section. Every paper starts on a new PDF page.

Write for the Non-expert audience specified in the selected profile. Give the
semantic introduction, notation support, careful qualification, prose,
statement-box, equation, figure, and bibliographic standards that method
requires, but do not add sections outside the collection structure.
In particular, give each paper's Main results or Main findings the selected
solo method's full conceptual explanation; the three-part collection shape
does not call for a compressed results summary.

For each applied paper, follow the selected profile's emphasis. Explain the
model-to-application bridge intuitively where it matters, without forcing a
fixed sequence of topics into the compact collection format.

Apply the selected audience and source rules to each mini-companion and to
How the papers are related. Introduce each paper's own actors and objects;
do not assume that a term has the same meaning in another paper. Keep the
three required subsections within each individual paper's mini-companion.

Research each paper's history enough to explain its place in the subject and
check its bibliography for consequential omissions. A plausible reference may
be included with a clearly provisional connection, but it cannot support an
unchecked technical, influence, or priority claim.
