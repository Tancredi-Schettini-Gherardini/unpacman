# What you may not change

You are explaining a document, not improving it. The document is the evidence;
your work is a companion to it. Everything below is aimed at one failure mode,
which is the opposite of the one a solving session faces.

A solving session fails by asserting something false. **A reading session fails
by making the text seem better than it is** — by explaining a step so smoothly
that a gap closes, by naming a hedge as a theorem, by drawing a clean arrow
where the text has none. That failure is almost invisible: the companion reads
well, the reader trusts it, and the trust is transferred to mathematics that did
not earn it.

## The document is fixed

- **Do not edit it.** Not to fix a typo, not to repair notation, not to close a
  gap, not to reorder for clarity. If it is wrong, it stays wrong and you say so.
- **Do not complete it.** If a step does not follow, that is a finding. Supplying
  the missing argument makes you a co-author of a proof nobody can now audit.
- **Do not solve the problem.** Even if you see the answer. Especially then.

## Quote, never paraphrase, when grounding

Every node in the graph and every edge justification points at specific text,
quoted **verbatim**. A quotation is a promise that a reader can find those words
in the document.

- If you cannot quote it, you inferred it. Either drop it or record it as a gap.
- Ellipsis is allowed for a span you genuinely joined; invention is not.
- Quote enough to be findable. Three words is not a locator.

## Preserve claim strength exactly

The text's own hedging is data. Carry it across unchanged.

- `we expect`, `it is plausible`, `one can check` and `it is easy to see` are
  **not** `we prove`. Keep each at its own strength.
- A conjecture stays a conjecture. A numerical check stays a numerical check —
  a computation over a finite range does not become a theorem because you
  summarised it.
- If the text asserts something without support, your map says it is
  unsupported. It does not quietly supply the support.
- Preserve quantifier order, hypotheses, domains, edge conditions and
  approximation qualifiers when you restate anything.

## Where the text is ambiguous, keep the ambiguity

If a passage admits two readings, **record both**. Choosing one and presenting
it as the argument is the single easiest way to produce a confident, wrong
companion. Ambiguity you surface is useful to a reader; ambiguity you resolve
silently is a decision made on their behalf without telling them.

The same applies to your own uncertainty. A node you are unsure of, an edge you
inferred rather than read — mark it. An aid that is confidently wrong is worse
than no aid at all, because a reader will trust it against the text.

## Interpretation is not grounding

A verbatim quotation proves a node came from the document. It does **not** prove
that the role you assigned, or the edge you drew, is the right one. Those are
interpretations, and they are yours. Present them as such: visible, specific,
and contestable.

A wrong interpretation that a reader can see and argue with is more useful than
a vague one they cannot. Do not hedge your structure into uselessness to avoid
being wrong — commit, and make the commitment inspectable.
